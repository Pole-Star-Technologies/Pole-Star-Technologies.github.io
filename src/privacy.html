5. Transcription, AI summaries and Google Gemini

{{app.name}} offers two ways to turn speech into text.

5.1 Offline (on your device)

You download a Whisper speech model (tiny or base) once. It runs on your phone. In this mode the audio is not uploaded for transcription. Each model file is checked against a published SHA-256 digest before use, so a tampered download is rejected rather than installed.

5.2 Cloud (Google Gemini)

In cloud mode, the audio for that meeting is sent from your phone to our own service, and our service passes it to the Google Gemini API for transcription, then sends the transcript back through Gemini to produce the minutes. Recordings up to 12 MB per part travel in the request itself. Larger recordings are placed in our private storage bucket and our service pulls them back by a short-lived signed URL (10 minutes) to send to Gemini; those stored parts stay in our storage until you delete the meeting’s data or your account (§9).

Models used: the Gemini 2.5 flash family. We do not use your audio or text to train any model ourselves, and we do not build a corpus of customer meetings.

Google processes that audio and text under its own Gemini API terms, including its own retention of API traffic. We do not control Google’s retention settings, so the honest statement is: anything you transcribe in cloud mode is visible to Google’s processing systems, and Google’s terms govern what happens to it there. If that is not acceptable for a given meeting, use offline mode.

5.3 Guard rails on the AI path

  • Request size limits, so one caller cannot make our service hold an arbitrarily large blob in memory.
  • Our service refuses to fetch any URL we did not issue, which closes the “make the server fetch an internal address” door.
  • Allowances are enforced server-side against the audio actually received, not against what the app reported.
  • Sign-in is required for every AI call; anonymous use is not possible.

6. Recording other people — your obligations

You, not we, are responsible for recording lawfully. Before you record a meeting you are responsible for telling the other participants and, where your jurisdiction requires it, getting their consent. Laws differ: some places allow single-party consent, some require everyone’s consent, and workplace rules may add more.

You must not use {{app.name}} to record conversations you are not a genuine participant in, to record confidential proceedings, or in any way that breaks the law or a duty you owe to another person. Deliberate misuse is a breach of our terms and grounds for suspending the account.

We process meeting content only on your instruction and only to produce the recording, transcript and minutes for you. If you receive a request from someone recorded in one of your meetings, delete that meeting in the app and contact us if it was cloud-transcribed.

9. Deleting your account and your data

In one place: open the app, go to Profile → Delete account, and confirm. This is available in the current released build; you do not need to email us, though you can ask us to delete an account by email from the address on the account.

What happens, in this order:

  1. Your audio is removed from our storage bucket.
  2. Your sessions, usage counters, device authorisations, entitlement and purchase events, minutes and meeting rows are deleted.
  3. Your profile and the account itself are deleted.

The order is enforced by the server on purpose: the deletion of your account refuses to run while your audio is still sitting in our storage. “Your account is gone” can never mean “your audio is orphaned in a bucket”.

Effect: you are signed out, you lose access to your meetings on every device, and the deletion is immediate. Backups rotate out afterwards (§8). If you only want a single meeting gone, delete that meeting in the app and, if it had been cloud-transcribed, tell us via {{org.privacyEmail}} so we can clear the server-side parts.

To stop billing, cancel in Google Play (Play Store → Subscriptions). Deleting your account does not cancel a subscription that Play is still billing.

10. How we protect your data

  • TLS on every request; the app talks to our services and to Google only over HTTPS.
  • Row-level security on every table and every storage path: your rows are reachable only by you.
  • The recordings bucket is private. Access is by short-lived signed URL, issued to the account that owns the object.
  • The Gemini API key lives only in our server environment, never in the app.
  • Session tokens are held on your device in the OS-protected keystore-backed store, and only their digest is stored by us.
  • Function permissions are scoped: anonymous keys cannot execute the functions that act on a named user.
  • Downloaded AI models are verified against a SHA-256 digest before they are installed.
  • Release builds do not log personal data such as coordinates or meeting content.

No system is unbreakable. If we ever have to tell you about a breach affecting your data, we will notify you and, where required, the relevant authority, as soon as we reasonably can.

12. Your rights and complaints

Depending on where you live you may have rights to access, correct, export, restrict, or object to processing, and to erase your data. {{app.name}} is built so that most of this is self-service: the app shows your meetings, your profile, your sessions and devices, and one action deletes all of it.

  • Copy of your data: the app itself (export/share a meeting), or ask us and we will provide what we hold for your account in a machine-readable form.
  • Correction: edit the meeting title and description in the app; profile fields come from your Google account.
  • Deletion: §9, in the app.
  • Anything else: {{org.privacyEmail}}, from the email address on the account so we can know whose request it is. We answer within 30 days, and usually much sooner.

Applicable frameworks we intend to satisfy: India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, and, where they apply to you, the EU/UK GDPR and the California CCPA. If you are in the EEA/UK and think we have mishandled your data, you may also complain to your supervisory authority; in India, to the Data Protection Board of India.
